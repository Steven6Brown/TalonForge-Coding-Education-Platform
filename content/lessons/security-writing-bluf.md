## Write for the reader's time

Security reports go to people deciding quickly: executives, legal teams, engineers. Make the main point impossible to miss.

## BLUF: Bottom Line Up Front

Start with the conclusion or the action you need, then give the evidence.

- Weak: "Over the past week we reviewed logs from multiple servers and observed several things..."
- Strong: "Block IP 203.0.113.45 today: it made 23 failed SSH logins on web01 and is likely guessing passwords."

A **headline** names the topic and key elements in one line; a **BLUF** states the point and the needed action.

## Clear writing habits

- Write out an acronym the first time (Bottom Line Up Front, BLUF), then use the short form.
- Prefer active voice ("The team contained the incident").
- Keep subjects and pronouns clear; check singular versus plural.
- Aim for a readable level and use plain words (readability tools such as Flesch-Kincaid help).

## Credible sources

Check for bias, strong emotion and click-bait. Treat vague attributions ("experts say", "critics say") as red flags until you find who and where. Confirm important facts with at least two reliable sources.

## APA basics

Double-spaced, 1-inch margins, page numbers top right, a title page, and in-text citations like (Author, Year, p. #) for quotes. A reference list is alphabetized by last name, with a hanging indent.

## Key facts this lesson's quiz checks

- **Acronyms.** The first time you use one in a document, **write out the full term with the acronym in parentheses**: "Business Impact Analysis (BIA)". After that you may use the acronym alone. Readers from other teams should never have to guess what an abbreviation means.

## Worked example: rewriting with BLUF

**BLUF** means *Bottom Line Up Front*: the main point goes first.

**Before (buried):**

> Over the past quarter the team has been reviewing several aspects of our remote access arrangements, including VPN configurations, user onboarding and the logging pipeline. During this review we noticed a number of issues, and one of them concerns our VPN gateway, which has not been updated for some time...

**After (BLUF):**

> **Bottom line:** The VPN gateway is running software with a known critical flaw and must be patched this week. Without the patch an attacker could gain access to the internal network. **Action needed:** approve a two-hour maintenance window on Thursday night. Details follow.

The reader learns the decision and the stakes in the first two sentences.

## Headlines

A good headline states the point, not the topic. Weak: "VPN Review". Strong: "VPN gateway needs critical patch by Friday".

## Acronyms

First use: write the full term with the acronym in parentheses, "Business Impact Analysis (BIA)". After that, the acronym alone is fine.

## Credible sources

Prefer **primary** sources (the vendor advisory, the court record) over summaries of summaries. Check who wrote it, when, and whether others confirm it. State how sure you are.

## Quick checklist before sending

- Is the bottom line in the first sentence?
- Does the reader know what you want them to do?
- Are acronyms defined?
- Is every claim traceable to a source?
